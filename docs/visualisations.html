Reading “The Gift of the Magi” with an LLM: how language turns loss into value

Hover over any element for the phrases behind it. Use the toolbar at the top right of each chart to zoom or save a PNG.
Research question. How does the narrator's language turn economic loss into emotional value, and what does the reader learn to call "wise"?
Claim tested. Words of shame, counting and selling are concentrated in the first two stages of the story, while words of wisdom and the narrator's comments are concentrated in the last stage.

Visualisations

Figure 1. Where does each dimension appear in the story?

How to read this: Each row is one dimension and each column is one segment of the story, in order. The number in each square is the number of hits; the shade shows hits per 100 words, so short and long segments can be compared. Dotted red lines mark where a new stage begins.

What it shows: Shame vocabulary is densest in segment 1 (12.4 hits per 100 words) and is absent from segment 9 onwards; corporeal vocabulary peaks at the sale (segment 5, 10.0) and again at the confession (segment 9, 6.8); objects and feeling peak when the combs are opened (segment 11, 12.0); inversion peaks only in the final paragraph (segment 13, 13.1).

Figure 2. How do the stages differ?

How to read this: The story is grouped into four stages. Each bar shows hits per 100 words, so a longer stage does not look stronger only because it is longer.

What it shows: Shaming falls from 5.86 to 0.00 hits per 100 words across the four stages, corporeal commodification peaks in Stages II and III (3.08 and 2.88), and objects and feeling and inversion both peak in Stage IV (7.33 and 4.02).

Figure 3. How does each dimension change across the story?

How to read this: Each line is one dimension followed across the segments in story order, in hits per 100 words. A rising line means that the dimension grows denser as the story moves on.

What it shows: Only shaming declines steadily (Spearman rho = -0.81); the other three dimensions move in bursts tied to scenes: the sale, the confession, the opening of the combs and the final paragraph.

Figure 4. Which phrases carry the most weight?

How to read this: The fifteen most frequent phrases in the extraction, coloured by dimension.

What it shows: "hair" is the most frequent single item (9), followed by "beautiful" (5) and "sold", "combs" and "gifts" (4 each): the vocabulary of value is concentrated in a few words about the body and the gifts.

Method note

Phrases were extracted by Claude (Anthropic), claude.ai chat from a prompt written by the researcher, verified word for word against the text, and matched by whole words, longest phrase first, without counting any stretch of text twice. Tests are descriptive: hits within a passage are not independent.
